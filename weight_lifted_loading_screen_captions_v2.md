# Total Volume Lifted — Scalable Loading-Screen Caption System

Designed for a **one-year training horizon**. Instead of only comparing cumulative volume to heavier and heavier objects, captions rotate through multiple comparison families so milestones stay fresh.

## How to scale it

Treat `V` as lifetime/year-to-date training volume in **kg** (sum of load × reps).

Each caption belongs to a comparison family. The app can select a family appropriate to the current magnitude, calculate the comparison, and round it to a fun human-readable number.

| Class | Typical range | Best comparison styles |
|---|---:|---|
| Spark | 0–2,500 kg | groceries, luggage, push-up equivalents, stairs |
| Momentum | 2,500–10,000 kg | people, furniture, wheelbarrows, small animals |
| Builder | 10,000–30,000 kg | cars, pianos, vending machines, daily averages |
| Heavy | 30,000–75,000 kg | elephants, vans, shipping pallets, repetition equivalents |
| Monster | 75,000–150,000 kg | buses, houses, aircraft, weekly/monthly transformations |
| Titan | 150,000–300,000 kg | whales, locomotives, monuments, absurd rep counts |
| Mythic | 300,000–600,000 kg | jets, space hardware, fleets, city-scale comparisons |
| Year Boss | 600,000–1,000,000+ kg | skyscraper-scale material, huge fleets, lifetime-style totals |

### Useful comparison formulas

These are intentionally approximate. For bodyweight-dependent comparisons, the examples assume an **80 kg person** and can be personalised if bodyweight is known.

- **Push-up equivalent:** one push-up moves roughly 65% of bodyweight → ~52 kg at 80 kg bodyweight. `pushups ≈ V / 52`
- **Bodyweight equivalents:** `V / 80`
- **20 kg plate equivalents:** `V / 20`
- **Barbell-only equivalents:** `V / 20`
- **25 kg suitcase equivalents:** `V / 25`
- **15 kg grocery-bag equivalents:** `V / 15`
- **100 kg refrigerator equivalents:** `V / 100`
- **250 kg grand-piano-ish equivalents:** `V / 250`
- **1,200 kg small-car equivalents:** `V / 1200`
- **1,500 kg average-car equivalents:** `V / 1500`
- **5,000 kg elephant-ish equivalents:** `V / 5000`
- **12,000 kg city-bus equivalents:** `V / 12000`
- **30,000 kg humpback-whale-ish equivalents:** `V / 30000`
- **Daily average over a year:** `V / 365`
- **Weekly average over a year:** `V / 52`
- **Monthly average over a year:** `V / 12`

# Dynamic Caption Families

For a scalable implementation, don't hard-code only milestone strings. Generate captions from templates and rotate families.

## 1. Rep-equivalent captions

Best for making huge totals feel relatable to exercise.

- `V / (0.65 × bodyweight)` → push-up equivalents
- `V / bodyweight` → bodyweight squat-equivalents by moved mass
- `V / 20` → empty Olympic-bar lifts
- `V / 60` → hypothetical 60 kg reps
- `V / 100` → hypothetical 100 kg reps

Examples:

- "That's the moved mass of **{pushups} push-ups**."
- "Equivalent moved mass to **{reps} reps at 100 kg**."
- "You could split that into **{reps} sixty-kilo reps**. Please don't do them in one set."

## 2. Everyday-object captions

Best below ~30 tonnes.

Objects: grocery bags, suitcases, washing machines, refrigerators, sofas, vending machines, pianos.

Template:

> "That's about **{count} {object}s** worth of mass. {punchline}"

## 3. Vehicle captions

Best from ~1 tonne upward.

Objects: small cars, average cars, vans, buses, loaded lorries.

Rotate punchlines rather than endlessly saying "you lifted X cars."

- "Your training log is now a parking lot."
- "That's rush-hour volume."
- "Your total needs a driving licence."
- "At this point we're measuring gains in traffic."

## 4. Animal captions

Best from ~5 tonnes upward.

Objects: horses, rhinos, elephants, whales.

Use sparingly so elephant comparisons remain funny rather than becoming the entire system.

## 5. Calendar transformations

Especially useful because the target horizon is **one year**.

Convert the same total into:

- kilograms per training day
- kilograms per calendar day
- tonnes per week
- tonnes per month
- volume accumulated per quarter

Examples:

- "That's **{daily} kg every day for a year**."
- "You've averaged **{weekly} tonnes per week**."
- "Your average month moved **{monthly} tonnes**."
- "At this pace, gravity gets no annual leave."

## 6. Streak / pace captions

These depend on both `V` and elapsed days `D`.

- `daily_rate = V / D`
- `year_projection = daily_rate × 365`
- `days_to_million = (1,000,000 - V) / daily_rate`

Examples:

- "At your current pace, you're heading for **{projection} tonnes this year**."
- "You've averaged **{daily_rate} kg of volume per day** since you started."
- "Keep this pace and the million-kilo club is about **{days} days away**."

These are much more engaging than fixed object comparisons because they react to the player/user.

## 7. Percentage-to-landmark captions

Create long-term targets and show progress toward them.

Suggested annual landmarks:

- 10,000 kg — **10-tonne club**
- 50,000 kg — **50-tonne club**
- 100,000 kg — **100-tonne club**
- 250,000 kg — **Quarter-million club**
- 500,000 kg — **Half-million club**
- 1,000,000 kg — **Million-kilo club**

Examples:

- "You're **73% of the way to 100 tonnes**."
- "Only **18.4 tonnes** until the Quarter-Million Club."
- "Million-kilo quest: **42% complete**."

## 8. Time-machine captions

Translate cumulative volume into what it would mean if performed over a ridiculous shorter period.

- per hour over a 24-hour day
- per minute over an 8-hour workday
- per second over one hour

Examples:

- "To move this in one day, you'd need to average **{kg_hour} kg every hour for 24 hours**."
- "Compressed into an 8-hour shift, that's **{kg_min} kg every minute**."
- "Your year of training does not fit comfortably into a Tuesday."

## 9. Gym-world captions

Keep comparisons inside lifting culture.

- number of 20 kg plates
- number of loaded 100 kg barbells
- number of 140 kg deadlifts
- number of 60 kg bench reps
- number of 100 kg squats

Examples:

- "That's **{count} imaginary 100 kg reps** hiding inside your training log."
- "Your total equals **{plates} twenty-kilo plates** moved once each."
- "Stack those 20 kg plates conceptually and the gym manager starts asking questions."

## 10. Meta / absurd captions

Use these between factual comparisons so the system doesn't feel like a unit converter.

- "Gravity has reviewed your account. It has concerns."
- "Your training log is becoming an infrastructure project."
- "Somewhere, Newton is updating the spreadsheet."
- "This stopped being exercise math several tonnes ago."
- "You are now measuring workouts in freight."
- "The loading screen needed bigger numbers because of you."
- "At this point, 'total volume' sounds less like fitness and more like shipping."
- "Your calculator has started adding commas automatically."
- "Gravity: undefeated. You: making it work for the win."
- "Achievement unlocked: statistically unreasonable amount of iron moved."

# Recommended yearly rotation

To avoid repetition, weight the caption generator differently as volume increases:

| Annual volume | Object | Exercise equivalent | Calendar/pace | Landmark | Absurd/meta |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| <10k kg | 40% | 30% | 10% | 10% | 10% |
| 10k–50k | 30% | 25% | 20% | 15% | 10% |
| 50k–150k | 20% | 25% | 25% | 20% | 10% |
| 150k–500k | 15% | 20% | 30% | 20% | 15% |
| 500k+ | 10% | 15% | 30% | 25% | 20% |

The important design change is that **the number gets bigger, but the joke doesn't have to**. At high volume, comparisons should increasingly describe pace, consistency, milestones, and absurd transformations instead of simply finding a larger object.
